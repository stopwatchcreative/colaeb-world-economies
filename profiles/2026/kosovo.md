Kosovo's US$ GDP rose 11.2% to US$12.5 billion in 2025, but real growth was +3.6%; most of the dollar gain was exchange-rate and price effects, not output. The economy held 150th place, between Equatorial Guinea and Togo.

Services make up 46.2% of output, industry 25.0% and agriculture 7.4%, with manufacturing at 12.5%. Inflation stood at 3.9%, and government gross debt is a low 17.5% of GDP. With 1.6 million people, GDP per capita of US$7,899 ranks 99th, and 104th on a PPP basis, well above the economy's overall rank.

Over the long run, Kosovo's dollar economy has grown 5.6 times since 2000, when it measured US$2.2 billion and ranked 143rd. It is now about the size of the Jacksonville, NC metro area and the 48th-largest of 52 economies in Europe & Central Asia. The IMF projects GDP of US$19.1 billion by 2030, a +53% rise that would lift it to 141st.
