# The world economy hit $118 trillion in 2025. Here's who moved.

**Colaeb's first World Ranking of Economies ranks 196 economies by GDP, and the year's biggest stories came from exchange rates as much as growth.**

The global economy reached US$118.35 trillion in 2025, according to the inaugural World Ranking of Economies, a new annual report from Colaeb that ranks 196 economies by GDP using World Bank and IMF data.

The top of the table holds steady. The United States produced 26.00% of world output on its own, at US$30.77 trillion, and together with China accounts for 42.47%. The ten largest economies hold 66.3% of the total. The smallest 100 combined hold 1.42%.

The closest race is for fifth. The UK edged back ahead of India, US$4.00 trillion to US$3.96 trillion, even though India's economy grew 7.6% in real terms against the UK's 1.4%. It may not last: the IMF's latest forecasts have India passing both the UK and Japan in 2027.

The trillion-dollar club also got bigger. Switzerland and Poland both crossed US$1 trillion, bringing the count to 21.

Lower down the ranking, the biggest moves had less to do with output than with currencies. Ghana climbed 10 places to 71st, the largest gain in the report, as its GDP measured in dollars jumped 37.1% on real growth of 6.0%. Ethiopia went the other way: real growth of 9.8%, among the fastest anywhere, but a 15.6% drop in dollar terms that pushed it down 7 places. Venezuela fell furthest, down 12 places, with consumer prices up 252.0%.

Taken over a longer stretch, China's economy is 15.9 times its 2000 size in current dollars. And the gap between the richest and poorest economies per person remains vast: US$218,978 in Liechtenstein against US$234 in Burundi.

The full report, at colaeb.com/world-economies, includes an interactive globe, profiles of every economy, side-by-side comparisons and a free downloadable dataset. Colaeb plans to publish it every year.
