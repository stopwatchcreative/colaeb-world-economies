## Summary

Colaeb's inaugural World Ranking of Economies ranks 196 economies by 2025 GDP in current U.S. dollars, using World Bank and IMF data. World output reached US$118.35 trillion. The United States produced 26.00% of it, and the top 10 economies together produced 66.3%. The UK edged back past India into fifth place, US$4.00 trillion to US$3.96 trillion, even though India's real economy grew faster; the IMF projects India will overtake both the UK and Japan in 2027. Switzerland and Poland crossed US$1 trillion, taking the trillion-dollar club to 21. The edition's biggest rank moves, Ghana up 10 places and Venezuela down 12, were driven mainly by exchange rates and prices rather than output. The report includes an interactive globe, profiles of every economy, side-by-side comparisons and a free downloadable dataset, and will be published every year.

## Ten key findings (quotable)

1. The world economy reached US$118.35 trillion in 2025, in current U.S. dollars.
2. The United States alone produced 26.00% of world output; with China, the two account for 42.47%.
3. The UK retook fifth place from India, narrowly: US$4.00 trillion to US$3.96 trillion, despite India's +7.6% real growth against the UK's +1.4%.
4. The IMF projects India will pass both the UK and Japan in 2027, reaching US$4.52 trillion.
5. The trillion-dollar club grew to 21 as Switzerland and Poland crossed US$1 trillion.
6. Currency, not output, drove the biggest climb: Ghana rose 10 places to 71st as dollar GDP jumped 37.1% on real growth of 6.0%.
7. Ethiopia grew 9.8%, the 6th-fastest rate in the world, yet fell 7 places as its GDP in dollars dropped 15.6%.
8. Ten economies hold 66.3% of world output; the smallest 100 together hold 1.42%.
9. China's economy is 15.9 times its 2000 size, up from US$1.22 trillion to US$19.50 trillion.
10. Per person, output ranged from US$218,978 in Liechtenstein to US$234 in Burundi, a 936-fold gap.

## Method in one paragraph

Economies are ranked by nominal GDP in current U.S. dollars for calendar year 2025. Figures come from the World Bank's World Development Indicators; where 2025 GDP is missing there, the IMF World Economic Outlook (October 2026) is used and labeled (nine economies, plus Taiwan). Forecasts and government debt come from the IMF. Movement compares 2025 with 2024 ranks. The full methodology, source list and retrieval dates are on the report page.
